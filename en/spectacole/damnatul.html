About the performance

“The Damned”, the new production of the Microbis dance company, directed and choreographed by Hugo Wolff, portrays humanity in all its complexity: a being that oscillates between contradictory states — happiness, fulfilment and love, pain, despair and resignation.

Two worlds take shape in the performance: the eternal world of vampires and the limited world of mortals. The story unfolds in several scenes, each supported by musical fragments specific to the moment portrayed. At first, the vampires — creatures thousands of years old — seek out mortals to turn them into vampires. Then the ritual of a mortal’s transformation is shown. The journey is rendered through slow movements, with pain conveyed through facial expression.

In another scene, the dance of two mortals is passionate and full of sensuality, sketching the love that envelops them. The vampires’ intervention, however, proves fatal for the lovers: the man is caught and turned into a vampire. The ritual on the cross is symbolic and leads towards “prehistoric times”, suggesting resurrection. The man becomes eternal, but he is punished: he can know love only as long as the woman lives. He finds his beloved again in another historical moment and in another guise, but lacks the strength to carry her into eternity.

“The Damned” thus depicts suffering — man’s punishment of remaining alone, isolated, thirsting to find his ideal. At the same time, this theatre dance performance brings out the idea that love has no limits: the feeling can exist between vampires, between mortals, or between vampires and mortals. In the end we are left with a question: is it better to live forever but know true happiness only for a short while, or to be mortal and enjoy the happiness offered by one’s ideal partner for the rest of one’s life?
