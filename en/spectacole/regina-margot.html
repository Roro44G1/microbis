About the performance

Understanding the reality of the reign of King Charles IX and reflecting it through dance is the essence of the performance “Queen Margot”. The night of 23 to 24 August 1572, known in history as the St Bartholomew’s Day Massacre, is the element on which the entire performance is built. A contextual and sequential understanding of the events offers a chilling perspective on one of history’s atrocities and a disturbing portrait of the moral bankruptcy that pervaded the whole of 16th-century society. The royal palaces are turned into a nest of vipers, of power politics, schemes and deceit.

The astonishing story of Queen Margot is transposed into dance through an oxymoron, with tableaux of dirty realism superimposed with beauty.

Context

The performance has a circular dramaturgy: it opens and closes with St Bartholomew’s Night.
