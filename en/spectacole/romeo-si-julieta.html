About the performance

Romeo and Juliet, or “the most beautiful and tragic love story of all time”, becomes in this adaptation a universe where words lose their intensity and are replaced by raw emotion. The main ingredient of this prescription is love — perhaps the most youthful and reckless component of human existence. Love makes you noble, yet it can just as easily stain you. Because of love we smile or suffer, we dream or fall, we are blinded by happiness or driven mad by restlessness. Its dosage is contagious; it can happen to anyone, at any time. Surely there must be a place where all these contradictory states coexist in harmony.

This time, we are not in Verona, but in an asylum, where reason dissolves and authentic feelings overwhelm the individual. In the white room, life is rewritten at maximum intensity: ecstatic joy collides with past dramas shaped by the outside world of rivalry, conflict and social status. In this world, two young people look at each other naively and fall instantly in love. A sweet cocktail of eroticism and desire floods their minds and souls — but when love meets impossibility, reason collapses. Emotional pain is amplified in confinement, and maintaining psychological balance becomes as impossible as walking a tightrope made of barbed wire.

The feelings and inner turmoil of the two lovers are carried by music and rhythm. There are moments when the lines of the body, the energy of movement, the abstract and the concrete merge, dissolving the need for words and creating a sea of images and messages charged with unsettling emotion. Storms of action and continuous waves of feeling follow one another on stage until, at last, silence descends. A painful silence. Love reveals itself as the most beautiful of feelings and the only one still capable of healing us — as individuals, as a collective, as a society. It is the only force that can mend us and bring us back — together.

Context

The performance was presented by the Microbis Association and the MICROBIS Theatre Dance Company, in partnership with the University Cultural Center of Sibiu.
